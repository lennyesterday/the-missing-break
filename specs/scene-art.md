# Second feature: scene art, characters, and walking transitions

## Why

The game should feel like the South Bronx in 1977. Every scene gets a picture, the player sees who is there, and when the crew moves between scenes they visibly walk there instead of the page just switching.

## What

- Every scene from the first feature (`hq`, `record_shop`, `block`, `station`) has a picture in `assets/scenes/<scene_id>.svg`, shown above the scene text. Flat poster style with a strong 1977 New York graffiti look: colourful pieces and tags on walls, shutters and doors, water towers on the skyline, and wherever the street is outside, the elevated subway in the back with a graffiti-covered train passing (looping animation).
- **The Basement (`hq`)** is the first scene picture (`assets/scenes/hq.svg`): the GoodFellas' hip-hop HQ after the break-in. It is **played inside the picture**: no title, text or Streamlit buttons under it (the inventory stays in the sidebar).
  - The room: a concrete floor with a little checkerboard dance floor, a proper sound system ("GOODFELLAS SOUND", woofers pumping to the beat), the graffiti corner with every crew member's tag (Needle, Bible 1, Meyhem, Reekz, Portals 77; Portals is spraying his), the crew's GOODFELLAS piece crossed out by the Sopranos, the Disco Fever battle flyer, a swinging bulb, a dripping pipe, feet passing the street-level window. Classic breaks lie all over the floor (*Apache*, *Funky Drummer*, *It's Just Begun*, *Impeach the President*, *Vitamin C*, *The Mexican*).
  - The stolen record: the empty BREAKS crate, and in front of it the empty sleeve of *Think About It* with a glowing dotted outline of the missing record over it.
  - Needle shakes with anger, steam over his head. Bible points at his rhyme book on the couch.
  - Reekz breaks on the dance floor in 3D, in a loop: a headspin, a crossfade, then a 1990: he spins in a one-handed handstand, his supporting hand staying put on the floor, body upside down above it, legs in a small V, the other arm tucked to his body; a faint dashed trail shows the circle his feet sweep. 48 positions per turn, knees and elbows as joints of their own (the windmill was removed), a crossfade, the headspin again. 36 positions per turn, black outlines on every limb.
  - The sound system is smaller (70 %), and three made-up 70s comic covers hang on the walls: "B-BOY BLASTER No. 1", "SPRAY CAN KID No. 7" and a comic "BLOCK PARTY" poster (no real comics).
  - **The classics on the floor can be played** (`logic.RECORDS`, each with a `song` slot for later): clicking a sleeve has Needle walk over, bend down and pick it up (the sleeve is gone from the floor), carry it to the right-hand turntable and put the record on. Then the picture switches by itself (`data-auto-action`) to **the turntable seen from above**: the record and its label spin (title, artist, year on the label), the tonearm sits in the groove, the strobe dots turn, and a NOW PLAYING panel shows title, artist, year and the song slot; **◀ BACK** returns to the basement. While the turntable is shown, that record's song plays (`static/audio/records/`, see `static/audio/CREDITS.md`). The turntable's round **START / STOP** button pauses it (the record and the notes stop, a ❚❚ PAUSED sign shows) and a second press goes on from the same spot. BACK always stops the song, also when it is paused, and as a safeguard the page stops a record's song whenever its turntable view goes away; the next record starts playing (not paused). (Scorpio was swapped for CAN's Vitamin C.) Once the crew has won "Think (About It)" back from King Krete, the empty pink sleeve at the crate is replaced by the full one, and Needle can put it on like the others (its song: `static/audio/records/think_about_it.mp3`); before that, clicking the empty sleeve only shows its sign. While Needle is busy nothing else can be clicked. Playing records is only for show: nothing is saved.
  - **Background sound:** while the basement picture is shown, its ambience loops softly (`static/audio/ambience/basement.wav`, generated from scratch by `art/ambience.py`: the hum of the sound system, a muffled boom-bap beat, water dripping, street noise, trains). It fades in over 1.5 s, fades out as soon as a record sleeve is pressed (and stays off on the turntable), comes back with ◀ BACK, and fades out when the crew leaves the basement. The 🔊 switch mutes it too.
  - **Voices:** a line that has a recording (`static/audio/voices/<scene>/<number>_<speaker>.mp3`, number = its place in the conversation from 00) is spoken when its bubble comes up: Needle's first line when the basement appears, the next one on the click to the next bubble. Lines without a recording stay silent. While someone speaks, the background sound is quieter. So far: `hq/00_needle.mp3`, `hq/01_bible.mp3`.
  - **The rhyme book's sign** has three buttons: **TAKE IT** (into the inventory, gone from the couch), **READ IT** and **LEAVE IT** (the sign closes, the book stays on the couch). The verse can't be listened to from the book (it only plays in round 2 of the rap battle).
  - **READ IT** opens MC Bible's view of his open rhyme book (`assets/screens/rhymebook_<n>.svg`): his hands in the leather jacket hold it, the Basement is dark around it, the pages are ruled paper with a red margin. The verse (`logic.RHYME_BOOK_VERSE`) is handwritten over four pages (two double pages) with crossed-out drafts, margin notes ("← save for round 2!!", "↑ Portals: draw the X here?", "Meyhem goes first, I finish him"), tags (Bible 1, GOODFELLAS), doodles (a crossed-out crown, stars), a coffee ring and his signature. A page flips over the spine when the book opens and on **PAGE ▶ / ◀ PAGE**; **CLOSE BOOK** returns to the Basement. Reading is only for show: nothing is saved.
  - **The conversation is clicked through:** one speech bubble at a time (`SCENES["hq"]["dialogue"]`); clicking it brings in the next person. After the last line it's gone. It plays once per game session.
  - **The ways out are graffiti buttons in the picture:** ◀ HIT THE BLOCK (bottom left) and RICO'S RECORDS ▶ (bottom right). They go through the game rules like any action.
  - **Things can be clicked:** the rhyme book and the empty sleeve open a sign with what it is and a song slot (`SCENES["hq"]["things"]`, `song` is empty for now: "♪ SONG: COMING SOON"). Looking at things or talking is not a move: nothing is saved.

  - **◀ BACK TO THE BLOCK** is a graffiti button in the picture.

The ball really goes through the hoop: the front of the rim and the net are drawn over the ball (the back of the rim behind it), so it drops into the rim and shows through the net, which stretches down for a moment (swish). The ball rolls back to its spot and the shooter goes back to his. Each shot plays once and is over by itself; it's only for fun: nothing changes, nothing is saved. Portals studies the Sopranos' piece from close up; Meyhem (mic) and Reekz (boombox) stand by. The station is not on the block any more: the **149 ST STATION ▶** button walks the crew to the station gate, key or not. Portals studies the tags from close up; the crew's conversation (`SCENES["block"]["dialogue"]`) is clicked through.

- The app shows "offer-<action>" parts of a picture while an item is still there to take, "has-<item>" while the crew carries it, "rejected-<action>" right after the rules turned an action down, and "done-<action>" right after an action with a message.
- **Graffiti lettering everywhere:** every piece in the game (train cars, titles, intro story, walls, buttons, the START OVER? sign) is painted with the graffiti font *Sedgwick Ave Display*: letters that bounce and lean a little, a black outline, a 3D block, an outer outline (white on big pieces, dark on small ones so light letters stay readable) and a colour fade. Tags use the handstyle *Sedgwick Ave*. Both fonts are SIL Open Font License, shipped in `static/fonts/` with their licence and served by Streamlit (`.streamlit/config.toml`: `enableStaticServing = true`), so they work offline. Small info lines (e.g. "YOUR SAVED GAME WILL BE REPLACED.") and speech bubbles stay in plain block letters for quick reading. Layouts use the real letter widths, measured from the font files (`art/fonts.py`, fonttools).
- All pictures are drawn inline by the svg_screen component, never with `st.image` (an SVG in `st.image` does not animate in the app).
- Characters are defined once in `logic.py` (`CHARACTERS`: id, name, element, portrait file) for Fantastic Needle, MC Bible, MC Meyhem, Portals, Reekz, King Krete, and Rico. Each scene lists which characters are present (e.g. `block`: Portals; `station`: King Krete; `record_shop`: Rico). The app shows them as small portraits with their names.
- Every character keeps the same look and props in every picture and animation:
  - **Fantastic Needle:** yellow shirt, red Kangol flat cap, red Pumas, headphones around his neck
  - **MC Bible:** black leather jacket over a green shirt, jeans, holds his rhyme book (cover reads "RHYME BOOK")
  - **MC Meyhem:** black leather jacket over a purple shirt, purple tracksuit pants, a microphone with cable in his hand
  - **Portals:** paint-splattered denim jacket, headband, always a spray can in his hand and a bag full of cans
  - **Reekz:** red tracksuit, white bucket hat, always carries his ghettoblaster on his shoulder
- Items get an icon in `assets/items/<item_id>.svg`, shown next to the item in the inventory.
- **Walking transitions:** after an accepted move between scenes, the app shows a short animation (about 4 seconds) of the whole crew (all 5) walking along the street from the old place to the new one. It plays at the top of the new scene, with the scene text and actions below it, and disappears with the next action. Transitions are stored as `assets/walks/<from>__<to>.svg`. If a transition file is missing, the new scene is shown directly.
- **Home screen:** the start/resume menu shows an animated title screen (`assets/screens/home.svg`): dusk in the South Bronx, the train passes on the el, Portals walks along the wall spraying "THE MISSING BREAK" letter by letter, the paint drips, a record with a piece broken out of it starts spinning, and "SOUTH BRONX · SUMMER 1977" appears. The rest of the crew stands by and nods to Reekz's ghettoblaster. It disappears once the game starts. There is no text and no Streamlit button below it; everything happens in the graphic.

Every line is a piece like the ones on the train (colour fade, black outline, 3D shadow). For the top lines he climbs a rolling ladder and pushes it along under each line; for the bottom lines he climbs down, rolls the ladder away and paints from the sidewalk. When he is done he lowers the can, glances back and runs off to the right, out of the picture: two 1970s NYPD officers (navy uniforms, peaked caps, badges) come running in from the left after him, one with a German shepherd on a leash, shouting "STOP! POLICE!" while the dog barks "WOOF!", and they run out to the right too:

  - then: *Two days before the borough DJ battle, the Sopranos stole GoodFellas crew's secret weapon: the record Lyn Collins – "Think About It". DJ Fantastic Needle saved this unheard break for the final. It's his secret joint.*
  - bigger: **HELP THE GOODFELLAS CREW TO GET BACK THE RECORD.**

When the game begins it fades out and the scene's own sound (e.g. the basement) fades in; on a scene without one, it just fades out.

- **Graffiti buttons on the train:** on the home screen the train pulls in and stops. Its three cars carry the pieces **START**, **RESUME** and **MENU**, and these are the buttons (mouse or keyboard):
  - **START** begins a new game. If a save exists, a graffiti sign appears on the home screen first: **START OVER?** *Your saved game will be replaced.* with the graffiti buttons **YES, START OVER** and **CANCEL** (PT01, A6). While the sign is up, the train's buttons do nothing. CANCEL keeps the save; YES resets to `hq` with an empty inventory and saves that.
  - **RESUME** continues the saved game where the player left off. Without a save it is greyed out and does nothing.
  - **MENU** is greyed out with a "SOON" sticker and does nothing yet. It gets its own spec later: articles that explain the hip-hop elements and teach hip-hop values.
- All SVGs are generated by `art/` (shared scenery and crew, so every character looks the same everywhere). Rebuild with `uv run python -m art.build`; never edit the files in `assets/` by hand.
- A rejected action shows no transition (it is not a move).
- The art is display only: it does not change the rules, the state, or what is saved.

Art for `battle` and the endings (these scenes don't exist yet), sound, the rhyme book, the vinyl, the tag decision, and the content of the MENU (hip-hop articles).

## Open questions for review

- Which transitions get their own walk? Proposal: one per route in the first feature (hq↔record_shop, record_shop→block, hq↔block, block↔station), plus a reusable generic street walk for any route without its own file.
- **Basement layout:** no button lies on a thing the player can click (the HIT THE BLOCK button keeps clear of the records), no two record sleeves lie on each other, and the sound system is small (half size) and stands against the back wall, clear of the records.
- **The cypher battle** (`assets/cypher/*.svg`, `art/cypher.py`; JOIN IN on the cypher): close up in front of the CORNER STORE, the crew from the block round the cardboard, *Apache* playing. Reekz battles **Kid Cyclone** (purple tracksuit), up to 3 rounds (ROUND n OF 3). Each round Kid Cyclone goes first with his set (`logic.RIVAL_SETS`); then Reekz steps onto the cardboard and the player picks his moves one by one (REEKZ'S NEXT MOVE?: TOPROCK, HELICOPTER, HEADSPIN, 1990, FREEZE; the helicopter, or coffee grinder: squatting low on one leg, hands on the floor, the other leg straight out sweeps round him twice, the support foot hopping over it, the hands lifting as it passes in front), his set so far listed at the top, until a FREEZE ends it (or 4 moves). The next move can be picked while he still does this one (UP NEXT: …, a change of mind is fine until it ends); when the move is over he goes straight into it, from the pose he ended in, with no stop. Without a pick he holds his last pose until one comes. During the last move of a set there is nothing to pick. The crowd judges (`logic.score_set`): every move scores points (power moves most), +1 FLOW for every step up the classic order (toprock → footwork → power → freeze), -2 for the same move twice in a row, -1 for a move shown in an earlier round, -2 without a freeze; the higher score takes the round (a tie goes to Kid Cyclone: it's his block). Two rounds win the cypher (REEKZ RUNS THE CYPHER! / KID CYCLONE KEEPS THE CYPHER). **In 3D, connected:** every move is drawn from one 3D skeleton (head, shoulders, elbows, hands, hips, knees, feet) in perspective, limbs passing in front of and behind the body. Every move starts and ends in a base pose: standing (toprock), down on his hands (helicopter, headspin, 1990) or the freeze; a move's clip starts from the pose the move before ended in, dropping down to the floor or getting up first when needed, so the moves flow into each other. ◀ THE BLOCK leaves at any time and stops the music. Only for show: nothing changes, nothing is saved.
